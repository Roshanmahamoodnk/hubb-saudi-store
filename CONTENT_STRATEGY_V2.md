# HUBB — the story inside the shell

Version 2 · 6 September 2026. This brief supersedes the earlier store content wherever it conflicts with the latest owner instructions. All Arabic is **Draft pending Saudi copywriter validation**. Public copy below uses the founder's account directly; internal evidence notes are separated from the narrative so the site can tell the story confidently and clearly.

## Current decisions

- HUBB is a Saudi manufacturer, as stated by the founder. The founder reports two years of R&D focused on a satisfying shell crack, good seed size, kernel crispness and flavour inside the seed, and developing the company's own method after boiling trials affected crunch.
- The four flavours remain **Classic Sea Salt / Garlic Salt / Pepper Lime / Fire Salt**. Earlier recipe proposals are superseded. Family Mix is the four-flavour assortment, never a fifth flavour.
- A single sachet is 30 g and **cannot be ordered individually online**. SAR 2.50 is a proposed physical-retail reference only. It must not appear as the online starting price, a shoppable variant, a checkout item or an online product offer.
- Online packs are Journey Cup, Family Mix and any retail-case format the final channel policy allows. Cup: five same-flavour 30 g sachets, 150 g. Family: twenty 30 g sachets, five of each flavour, 600 g. Retail case: twenty-four same-flavour 30 g sachets, 720 g.
- The site needs separate shopping, wholesale-order, distributor-partnership and creator/collaboration paths. Their forms and success messages should describe their actual next step.
- The current application proposal uses a SAR 39 minimum online merchandise subtotal, SAR 15 shipping below SAR 75 and free shipping from SAR 75 for eligible delivery areas. These are proposed commercial choices, not validated cost or coverage findings. Resolve the tokens in this document from the shared application policy so copy stays consistent.

## Finished founder story — English

### Two years, working on the little things.

We are a Saudi manufacturer, and HUBB began with a moment we know well: a gathering, a conversation, and a sunflower seed between your fingers.

We wanted to give that small moment more attention. The way the shell cracks. The size of the seed. The crispness of the kernel. And the flavour you find inside.

For two years, those details shaped our research and development. We tested, tasted and kept adjusting. In our early trials, we explored boiling the seeds with spices. In those trials, the kernel cooked further than we wanted and lost some of the crunch we were looking for. That experience pushed us to develop our own approach.

Today, that work is the story behind HUBB: four flavours, sealed little packs and a place at the gatherings that matter to us. Classic Sea Salt. Garlic Salt. Pepper Lime. Fire Salt.

We put care into the seed. You bring the company.

## Boiling and the Japanese attribution

The bounded primary-source search did **not** establish a Japanese traditional practice of boiling in-shell sunflower seeds with spices. Japan's Ministry of Agriculture describes seasoned preserved foods such as tsukudani, but that is not evidence for the specific sunflower-seed story. No cultural origin should be inferred from the existence of Japanese simmering techniques. [MAFF — seasoned preserved foods](https://www.maff.go.jp/e/policies/market/dento_syoku/bunrui/tyoumi-kakou.html)

A peer-reviewed study investigated boiling and roasting sunflower seeds under its own experimental conditions. It does not verify HUBB's method, Japanese provenance or a universal “boiling destroys crunch” claim. For the website, say **“In our early boiling trials…”**, preserving the founder's actual experience as the source. [Tenyang et al., Food Science & Nutrition](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/fsn3.2637)

Do not replace the unverified Japanese attribution with a Chinese attribution simply because a patent or recipe appears in search. The development story works without borrowing an external tradition.

## A real tasting-review programme

Invitation: **“Tell us what you noticed, in your own words.” / “قل لنا وش لاحظت، بكلامك أنت.”**

Ask people who actually tried the sample to identify its flavour/pack, tasting date and whether it was purchased or supplied. Keep design feedback separate from a product taste review. Honest sample recipients can leave useful feedback, but their reviews must never become “verified purchase” without an actual purchase link.

Neutral prompts:

1. What did you notice when opening the shell? / وش لاحظت وأنت تفتح القشرة؟
2. How did the size feel to you? / كيف كان حجم الحبّة بالنسبة لك؟
3. How would you describe the kernel's texture? / كيف توصف قوام اللبّ؟
4. What flavour did you notice after separating the kernel from the shell? / أي نكهة لاحظت بعد فصل اللبّ عن القشرة؟
5. What would you change? / وش الشيء اللي تحب نغيّره؟
6. Which flavour or pack would you choose again, if any? / أي نكهة أو عبوة ممكن تختارها مرة ثانية، إن وجدت؟

Use optional open text and a consistently defined rating scale if needed. Do not require “crunchy”, “perfect crack” or “flavour inside” in a review; do not pay only for positive feedback. Ask permission before public display. Show sample/gift relationships clearly. Do not publish an average until real ratings exist, and calculate it only from the displayed/relevant review set. Reviews about delivery or page design should not inflate a seed-taste rating.

Google requires review markup to represent real visible reviews of the specific item; use genuine reviewer information and no fabricated aggregate. Google recorded a July 2026 update concerning fake and undisclosed incentivized reviews, so a sample relationship should remain explicit. [Google — review snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet), [Google — documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
